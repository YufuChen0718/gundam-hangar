Summary

We started from a single static mesh generated with Meshy: about 1.86 million triangles, one 8K texture, and no skeleton at all. Since the proposal, we turned it into a robot you can pose and animate in the browser. Right now it has a 15-joint full-body rig, three actions (idle, aim, swing) that blend into each other, and a rifle, beam saber and shield that attach to sockets on the hands and forearm. The biggest change from our plan is the skinning: smooth LBS made the armor bend like rubber, so we switched to a rigid rig where every armor plate moves as one piece. Next we want to add spline interpolation, a simple IK solver, and a performance study.

Progress

Asset pipeline: decimation and texture baking

The raw mesh was way too heavy for the web, so we decimated it to about 30.7k triangles. This broke the texture: edge collapses merged vertices across UV seams, so the UVs got interpolated between unrelated islands and the shield star came out smeared. To fix it, we made a new UV altas for the low-poly mesh and baked the high-poly color, normal and metal/roughness maps onto it, which basically shoots a ray from each low-poly texel and copies what it hits on the original surface. One cage distance didn't work everywhere, so we used two passes, a tight one (5 mm ) for crevices and a wider one (15 mm) for deeper gaps. The final model went from 205 MB to 17.5 MB.

Full-body rig and forward kinematics

The pelvis is the root joint. The torso, head and arms hang under it, and so do the two legs, so each joint's world matrix is just its parent's world matrix times its own local transform. In total there are 15 body joints plus 6 small hinges for the skirt plates, with limits so the knees only bend one way (0°–55°) and the torso twists at most ±40°. When the robot crouches, we lower the pelvis by the right =mount so both feet stay on the platform; across our 9 test poses the feet move less than 1 mm.

Skinning: our LBS shader vs. rigid armor

We wrote our own linear blend skinning in the vertex shader, p' = Σ wk Mk p, using bone matrices from our FK code. It matches Three.js's built-in skinning to within 6 × 10-8 m, so we know the math is right. The problem is that LBS blends vertices near a joint between two bones, and that is exactly what hard armor should never do: the plates stretched and twisted, with mean edge strain up to about 10% in our extreme poses. So we built a rigid rig instead. We cut the mesh slong concave seams (a min-cut on the dual graph where concave edges are cheaper), used a single clean plane at the knees and waist, capped every cut, and added simple inner-frame cylinders and spheres at the joints to hide the gaps. Rigid mode runs the same shader with one bone per vertex, and the strain drops to basically zero.

Keyframe animation and blend vs. cut

Each action is stored as a few key poses. Every frame, we find the two keys around the current time, apply that segment's easing curve, and slerp each joint's rotation. When the user switches actions in the m iddle of one, we save the pose the robot is in right now and slerp from it to the new clip iver 0.3s with smoothstep weights. We wrote all of this ourselves instead of using Three.js's AnimationMixer. In the plot, a hard cut makes the shoulder jump 48° in a single frame, while the bland's first step is only about half a degree.

Socket equipment

Each weapon is its own model, and its world transform is Mjoint · Msocket · Moffset, where the offset fixes the item's own modeling frame (where the grip is, which way the barrel or blade points). The rifle and saber share the right-hand socket, and the shield sits on the ledt forearm. We checked that the scene-graph result matches the explicit matrix product (difference around 10-16 m), but that only tells us the code is consistent, so we still check the grip by eye.

Problems and fixes

The waist gave us the most trouble. Our first cut went through the top edge of the skirt armor, so twisting the torso tore that edge into thin silvers and exposed the flat caps. We moved the cut to a horizontal plane inside the red abdomen, extended the abdomen 1.6 cm down inside the skirt ring, recessed the caps, and added an elliptical frame piece. Now we see no holes at the waist anywhere from −40° to +40°. Our main takeway was that where you cut the mesh matters a lot more than how you blend it.
