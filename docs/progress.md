## 2026-11-25 · T16 leg IK rework: walk/lift in the leg plane, anatomical limits
- **Problem (user feedback):** feet could only slide on the floor, legs splayed sideways (circular 75° hip cone), and the ankle was forced flat ignoring its limit, so it looked stiff.
- **Fix:**
  - Feet are dragged in their leg's swing plane (hip→ankle line plus body forward): step forward/back or lift. They cannot go below the floor, and the handle snaps back onto the foot past the limits.
  - The hip uses an elliptical swing–twist limit (`clampSwingEllipse` in `ik.ts`): pitch in JOINT_LIMITS [−20°, 45°], sideways ±12°, twist ±15°.
  - The ankle uses its JOINT_LIMITS pitch plus ±8° yaw and ±12° roll, which keeps the feet flat and straight (0° tilt). Without the yaw, the slanted legs turn the feet 6.8° in a crouch.
  - Only grounded feet support the body (weight fades over a 4 cm lift). Pelvis follow is 40% fore–aft and 12% sideways, pelvis yaw is 30% (max 10°), and the automatic drop counts only grounded feet.
  - `FootLegStatus` reports the ankle's wanted Euler angles and the foot tilt.
- **Limits are the cap:** with the T13 hip (45°) and knee (55°) limits a foot lifts at most about 11 cm. A real high knee needs those raised, which needs an armor clipping check.
- **Checks:** `npm test` 30/30. In the browser, a real mouse lift is exact after the snap, the squat is exact, and the crouch has 0° foot tilt. `check:ik` and the build pass.

## 2026-11-25 · T16 leg IK: body follow, knee poles, Squat action
- **Body follow (`applyFootIk` in `src/animation/animator.ts`, `FOOT_IK_CONFIG`):** when ankles are dragged, the pelvis yaws 50% with the foot-to-foot line (torso counter-turns 60%), slides 60% of the feet-midpoint shift, and lowers by the closed-form minimum drop that keeps both ankles reachable: drop = max(requested, maxᵢ(vᵢ − √(R² − hᵢ²))). All terms are scaled by the IK weight and are zero when the feet are at their planted rest.
- **Knee poles:** purple draggable markers with a knee→pole line (`setFootPole`, `setFootPoleWorld`). "Re-plant feet · reset knees" makes them automatic again. Hip twist ±25° limits how far a knee can swing.
- **Squat action:** `Keyframe.pelvisDrop` (new clip channel, sampled with the joints' easing, blended across transitions via `startDrop`), and a `squatClip` (0 → 0.08 m → hold → 0, arms forward). `playAction("squat")` plants the feet and turns Leg IK on at full weight. The transition start pose is now taken from `this.pose` rather than the joints, so IK/body-follow offsets are not baked into blends.
- **Checks:** `npm test` 29/29 (body follow, spread-feet auto-drop, knee pole + hip twist limit, squat with feet planted on every frame). In the browser, squat ankle error ≤ 4.5e-16 m and spread feet are exact. `npm run check:ik` and `npm run build` pass with no console errors.

## 2026-11-15 · T14 Our own Linear Blend Skinning shader & CPU reference (M3)
- Renamed `mecha-right-arm.glb` / `.blend` to `gundam-rigged.glb` / `.blend` across the repository and updated all references.
- Implemented our own Linear Blend Skinning (`src/mecha/lbs.ts`) on plain `THREE.Mesh` objects using a custom `ShaderMaterial` (no `SkinnedMesh`, `Skeleton.update()`, or Three.js skinning shader chunks on the `"lbs"` or `"rigid"` paths) and added a 3-way toggle (**Three.js skinning / our LBS / rigid parts**) in the Inspect panel.
- **LBS Equation & CPU vs. GPU split:**
  - **CPU (per frame):** Our hierarchical FK computes each joint's world transform $W_j$. For each mesh part $m$ (with primary joint bind origin $\mathbf{o}_m$ and precomputed relative inverse bind matrices $\widetilde{B}_{m, j}^{-1} = B_j^{-1} T(\mathbf{o}_m)$), the CPU computes $M_{m, j} = W_{\text{root}}^{-1} W_j \widetilde{B}_{m, j}^{-1}$ and uploads the 15 matrices as `uniform mat4 uBoneMatrices[15]` (`Float32Array(15 * 16)`).
  - **GPU (vertex & fragment shaders):** The vertex shader reads per-vertex `skinIndex` and `skinWeight` attributes and evaluates $\mathbf{p}' = \sum_{k=0}^{3} w_k (M_{m, j_k} (\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{o}_m))$ and $\mathbf{n}' = \mathrm{normalize}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{3} w_k (\mathrm{mat3}(M_{m, j_k}) \mathbf{n})\big)$ (or $\mathbf{p}_{\text{rigid}}' = M_{m, \text{primary}(m)} (\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{o}_m)$ in `rigid parts` mode). The fragment shader samples the original 2K base-color texture (or part debug color) with key/fill directional + hemisphere lighting.
- **CPU LBS Reference & Evaluation (`docs/results/t14-evaluation.md`):** Implemented `LbsManager.cpuPosition` matching the shader math to recompute per-pose browser seam gaps ($4.47 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}$ to $9.54 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}$ across the 9 test poses, vs. $103\text{–}236\text{ mm}$ cracks in `rigid parts` mode) and per-vertex differences against Three.js skinning ($1.05 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}$ to $7.12 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}$ max diff across 11,694 skinned vertices). Saved shoulder/elbow comparison screenshots (`docs/results/t14-*.png`).
- **Known issues:** Linear Blend Skinning can exhibit slight volume loss ("candy-wrapper" effect) under extreme twisting; dual quaternion skinning is an optional comparison (M9).
